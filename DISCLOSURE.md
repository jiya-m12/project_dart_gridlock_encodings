This is a solo entry (team "Random Qubit", Jiya Maurya).

- **My earlier notebook:** https://github.com/jiya-m12/qubo-interbank-settlement (MIT). An earlier independent project: I took the use case (interbank settlement and counterparty netting) from Quantum Dice's Trinity 2026 Challenge, a probabilistic-computing challenge, and formulated it as a QUBO. The implementation was my own.
  - **What it already had:** a base formulation with the same objective and liquidity constraint as here (maximise settled value subject to each bank's net outflow ≤ liquidity); a standard binary-slack QUBO; IQP + Master-Satellite (IQPMS) penalties from De Santis et al. with LP-based coefficient minimisation, which I found work only on gridlock scenarios (this project's E1 uses plain IQP, without the Master-Satellite step); penalty weights λ_u = γ·Σw with γ = 2; greedy baselines; simulated annealing; and the 6-bank / 9-payment gridlock example (greedy settles 3 of 57, the optimum 54).
  - **What carries over here:** the problem formulation, the idea of applying IQP with coefficient minimisation, and the gridlock example (`gridlock_instance()` in `settle/instances.py`). Its CSV instances are used only to cross-check slack counts (`settle/_nbload.py`; the files are not shipped).
  - **No code from it is used.** Everything in this repository was written in the window.
- **Pre-window planning (with Claude, notes dated 9 Oct before 19:00 ET):** problem choice, prior-art notes, a written problem spec and a small exploratory check of qubit/gate counts and QAOA on a few toy instances. None of that code is used; every number here was regenerated by code written in the window.

## Methods from the literature (implemented from the papers, not from their code)
- IQP penalty polynomials: De Santis, Tirone, Marmi, Giovannetti, arXiv:2406.07681 (Sec. III A, Eqs. 10-13). Our addition: among valid polynomials with the fewest slack bits, choose the one with the smallest largest coefficient (MILP + exact LP polish).
- Local/neighbour/global penalty weights: same paper, Eqs. 27-29 (our neighbour rule follows Eq. 28; their Eq. 68 for MPBS double-counts shared payments and is more conservative).
- Unbalanced penalization: Montanez-Barrera et al., arXiv:2211.13914.
- Constant penalty per violated constraint (our E2) and the "virtual penalty" lambda*(violation)^2 (our E4): Bucher, Stein, Feld, Linnhoff-Popien, IF-QAOA, arXiv:2504.08663 (Sec. IV: the "Alternative Architectures" paragraph gives the constant penalty, and Eq. 18 the virtual penalty). E4's function is also Hess et al.'s classical sample score, arXiv:2403.18395, Eq. 11.
- CVaR objective: Barkoutsos et al., Quantum 4, 256 (2020).
- INTERP warm start: Zhou et al., PRX 10, 021067 (2020).
- Exact expansion of a Boolean penalty into Pauli-Z terms (E2, E4): Hadfield, arXiv:1804.09130; the same construction for custom penalties in QAOA: Lee, Goh & Lau, arXiv:2610.04908 (Walsh custom-penalty QAOA, October 2026). Compiling higher-order terms directly into QAOA gates: Campbell & Dahl, arXiv:2111.12754.
- Gray-code ordering of diagonal phase terms (noise check): Welch, Greenbaum, Mostame, Aspuru-Guzik, New J. Phys. 16, 033040 (2014); Bullock & Markov, QIC 4, 27 (2004). Native ZZ gates (noise check): IonQ native-gate documentation.
- Bech-Soramäki FIFO gridlock resolution (baseline): Bech & Soramäki (2001), implemented from the description.
- Product-state control (one RY per qubit, no entanglement): in the spirit of Q-INSP in Martini et al., arXiv:2501.08794. They used Bayesian optimisation and shots; we use COBYLA and exact probabilities.
- IQP minimality check (`scripts/check_iqp_minimal.py`): our own LP/MILP, written independently of `settle/iqp.py`.
